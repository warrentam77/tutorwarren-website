2 · How electricity produces and transfers heat

A resistive element converts electrical energy into thermal energy when current flows. The element’s resistance and the applied voltage determine the heating power.

P = VI = I²R = V²/R   ·   Energy (kWh) = power (kW) × time (h)

Conduction · contact

Heat passes through a solid: an immersion element’s sheath transfers heat to the water touching it; a heating cable warms the surrounding floor screed.

Convection · circulation

Warmer water or air rises as it becomes less dense; cooler fluid moves in to replace it. This spreads heat around a cylinder or room.

Radiation · rays

Infrared energy travels from a hot surface to other surfaces without needing a material medium. Radiant heaters warm people and objects directly.

Heat ≠ temperature: heat is energy transferred because of a temperature difference; temperature describes thermal state. Kelvin temperature = °C + 273.15. A change of 1 K equals a change of 1 °C.

Supply and isolation

Portable heaters use a suitable socket supply. Fixed equipment needs an appropriately designed circuit, connection and accessible isolation. Typical arrangements include fused connections for fixed direct heaters, adjacent double-pole isolation for storage heaters, and heat-resistant final connections. Cable sizes and protective-device ratings must be selected for the actual installation.

Traditional storage systems use a separately controlled off-peak supply. Current products and tariffs vary: confirm whether the heater requires an off-peak supply, permanent supply, or both. Size circuits from the actual load and installation conditions.

Bathroom electric underfloor heating needs the appropriate protective measures, including the specified earthed metallic covering/grid where required. Use the current requirements and the product instructions for the particular installation.

Memory hook: Direct = heat now. Storage = charge now, release later. Ducted = move warm air. Underfloor = warm the floor.

4 · Electric water heating

Point-of-use heater

Heats water close to a tap. A small storage type keeps a small volume warm; an instantaneous type heats flowing water and can have a much higher power rating. Supply arrangements follow the actual rating and manufacturer’s instructions.

Immersion heater

A resistance element sits inside a metal sheath immersed in cylinder water. The electrical conductor is insulated from the sheath; heat crosses the sheath into the water. Convection spreads heat through the stored water.

Direct versus indirect cylinder

Direct electric heating uses an immersion element. An indirect cylinder has a separate coil carrying boiler primary water; heat passes through the coil wall into domestic water. Many indirect cylinders also have a backup immersion heater.

An immersion heater serving a vessel exceeding 15 litres should have its own radial circuit. A typical domestic element is 3 kW. Separate the immersion circuit from the low-power boiler control circuit: they perform different jobs.

Where bathroom locations are involved, isolation position, RCD protection, bonding and equipment suitability must be assessed for the actual installation. Supplementary bonding is not decided simply by the presence of an immersion heater.

Worked example

A 3 kW heater supplied at 230 V draws I = 3000 ÷ 230 = 13.0 A. Operating at full power for 2 hours uses 6 kWh. This calculation does not, by itself, select the cable or protective device.

Extension: estimate water heating time

For 150 litres (approximately 150 kg) heated from 15 °C to 60 °C: Q = mcΔT = 150 × 4.18 × 45 = 28,215 kJ = 7.84 kWh. At 3 kW, ideal time = 7.84 ÷ 3 = 2.61 hours. Actual time is longer with heat loss and depends on draw-off and controls.

Thermostat principle: dissimilar metals expand by different amounts. In a bimetal strip or copper-tube/invar-rod device, temperature movement operates contacts. An electronic thermostat uses a sensor and switching electronics instead.

6 · S-plan — two separate two-port valves

These schematics use a conventional 230 V demand interface. Some boilers require volt-free or digital inputs, and some control their own pump overrun. Follow the exact equipment instructions for permanent supplies, pump connections, protection and isolation.

S-plan electrical connections

Each programmer channel and thermostat is in series with its valve motor. Both end switches receive permanent live on grey; their orange outputs join the demand node in parallel. A boiler or pump neutral connection is not a switched-live connection.

S = Separate valves. One normally closed two-port valve controls radiators; another controls the cylinder coil. Each has a motor, spring return and auxiliary end switch. Either circuit can run alone, or both can run together.

Boiler + pump → split flow → [CH two-port valve → radiators] and [HW two-port valve → cylinder coil] → common return → boiler

7 · Y-plan — one three-port mid-position valve

Y-plan electrical connections

Two distinct junctions must remain separate: the grey HW-not-required junction, and the orange/cylinder-CALL boiler-demand junction. The actuator’s complete internal circuit is represented as a functional block.

Y = one junction, two destinations. A conventional V4073A-style valve has inlet AB from the boiler/pump, outlet A to radiators, and outlet B to the cylinder coil. It selects B only, A+B, or A only.

Boiler + pump → AB [three-port valve] → A: radiators / B: cylinder coil → common return → boiler

Why the grey wire matters

When HW time is OFF, the programmer’s HW OFF terminal energises grey. When HW time is ON but the cylinder has reached temperature, the thermostat’s SATISFIED contact energises grey. With white also live, the actuator moves from mid-position to heating-only.

The valve is spring-biased towards B when fully de-energised, but can remain held towards A with grey live after a previous heating demand. The selector below describes steady operating demand modes, not the actuator’s complete internal switching or shutdown history.

Memory hook: White wants heating. Grey says hot water is not needed. Orange joins the boiler demand.

10 · Quick revision

Time → temperature → water path → heat

Programmer enables → thermostat calls → valve selects/opens → boiler and pump respond.

Immersion ≠ cylinder thermostat circuit

The immersion directly heats stored water electrically. The cylinder thermostat in an indirect system requests heat from the boiler.

S-plan

Two valves; brown opens; grey feeds switch; orange calls boiler.

Y-plan

One valve; B = hot water, A+B = both, A = heating. White = CH; grey = HW not required.
