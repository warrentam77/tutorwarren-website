LO1 – Legislation (7 questions)

Waste duty of care

Sort • Secure • Check • Record

Separate and contain waste; check that the recipient is authorised; keep the required transfer or consignment records.

Noise control

Quiet first, protection too

Reduce noise at its source and assess exposure. Hearing protection supports appropriate controls; it is not a substitute for controlling noise.

The environment = the world in which we live, work and play. Built environment = materials and resources we use to build things. Offences are either common law (damage, nuisance, negligence – compensation) or statutory (crimes – fines/prison).

LawWhat to remember
Environmental Protection Act 1990Integrated pollution control; cornerstone is BPEO (best practicable environmental option). 8 Parts (waste on land, nuisances, litter, nature conservation, contaminated land…). “Red List” = 23 substances (mercury, cadmium).
Hazardous Waste Regs 2005Discharge lamps/tubes (fluorescent, sodium, metal halide, mercury vapour) = hazardous. Batteries too. Hazardous waste marked with an asterisk; EA shows it in red.
Pollution Prevention & Control Regs 2000Replaced Integrated Pollution Control. Best Available Technique (BAT). Permits: A(1) = Environment Agency (greatest pollution potential); A(2) and Part B = Local Authority.
Control of Pollution Act 1989Controlled waste carriers must register with EA (England/Wales) or SEPA. Burning waste on site can contravene it.
Noise at Work Regs1989: 85 / 90 dB(A), peak 140 dB(A). 2006: first action level 80 dB(A), second 85 dB(A). Control noise at source; ear protection is a last resort. Hearing damage is cumulative; vibration white finger from vibrating tools.
Packaging (Essential Requirements) Regs 2003Packaging must be (1) minimum volume/weight needed for safety and (2) reusable or recyclable.
Environment Act 1975Created the regulatory bodies (EA, SEPA).
WEEE Directive 2007Makers/distributors must arrange recycling of waste electrical and electronic goods at end of life (July 2007).
Controlled Waste / Duty of CareDuty of care for all waste: pass only to authorised carriers, with Waste Transfer Notes; no time limit; segregate and label skips; don’t leave waste for others. Special waste (asbestos, lead-acid batteries, oil, solvents, pesticides) needs competent, licensed handling.

Ways work harms the environment: land contamination, air pollution, pollution of watercourses.

Building Regulations: Part P = electrical safety; Part L = conservation of fuel and power; Part C = site preparation/moisture; Part M = access; Part F = ventilation; Part E = sound. Notifiable (if not registered with a competent person scheme): new circuit, kitchens*, bathrooms, special locations (pools, saunas). Non-notifiable: replacing accessories, like-for-like cable repair, adding lighting points to existing circuits (not in special locations). *Book: England no longer treats kitchens as special locations; Wales does. Competent person schemes: BRE, BSI, ELECSA, NICEIC, NAPIT.

Code for Sustainable Homes: 1–6 star rating; Code 6 = zero carbon; zero-carbon new homes targeted for 2016.

Lamp recycling process: wholesaler delivers container → place tubes whole → wholesaler collects → company receives a Duty of Care Note (file it) → cost (~50p per 1800 mm tube) passed to customer.

LO2 – Reducing wastage (3 questions)

Waste hierarchy

Really Useful Resources Reduce Disposal

Reduce → Reuse → Recycle → Recover → Dispose. First avoid creating waste; disposal comes last.

Waste hierarchy (best → worst): Reduce/prevent → Re-use → Recycle → Recovery (energy capture) → Landfill/disposal. Landfill is the last resort.

  • Re-use: return part coils of cable and spare conduit, trunking, tray to the employer’s stores (credited to the job).
  • Prevent: good design, careful ordering, managed stock levels, modular/pre-assembled factory-tested units (less on-site fabrication).
  • Report any environmental hazard or incident to your supervisor – effects such as asbestos exposure are cumulative.
  • Eco measures: thermostatic controls, LED/CFL lamps, water and smart meters, insulation, repairing leaks, PIR/motion sensors.
  • Energy Performance Certificates grade buildings A (best) to G (worst) – from Part L.
LO3 – Environmental technology systems (10 questions)

Solar PV

PV = Produces Volts

Sunlight → PV cells → d.c. → inverter → a.c. Inverter: DC IN, AC OUT.

Solar thermal

Thermal = temperature

Sun → collector → circulating fluid → heat exchanger → stored hot water. Think hot water rather than electrical output.

Wind generation

Blades turn; generator earns

Moving air turns the rotor, which drives a generator. Think clear airflow; buildings can cause turbulence.

Hydroelectricity

Flow × Fall

Flow is water passing each second; head is the height difference. Both influence available power. Output depends on the water resource.

Heat pumps

Move heat; do not make it all

A heat pump transfers environmental heat indoors using electrical input. Air, Ground, Water = three heat sources.

Biomass

Bio = living origin; mass = fuel

Wood and other organic fuels release heat when burned. Remember fuel store + flue + fuel supply. Carbon impact depends on the full fuel lifecycle.

Combined heat and power

One fuel, two useful outputs

A single process provides electricity and useful heat. Recovering heat that would otherwise be wasted improves overall efficiency.

Rainwater harvesting

Roof → reservoir → reuse

Collect rainfall, store it and supply suitable non-drinking uses. Keep harvested water separate from drinking-water supplies.

Grey water

Bath to bathroom flush

Used bath, shower and wash water can be treated for suitable reuse. Grey is not black: toilet waste is excluded.

Energy hierarchy: 1 reduce demand → 2 use energy efficiently → 3 use renewables → 4 clean fossil fuel use. Microgeneration = heat up to 45 kW, electricity up to 50 kW. Feed-in Tariff: generation tariff + export tariff, tax-free, guaranteed 25 years; installer must be MCS-accredited. Smart meters are two-way, used with microgenerators.

Heat pump cycle: Every Clever Cat Explores

E → C → C → E: Evaporator → Compressor → Condenser → Expansion valve.

1. Evaporator: refrigerant absorbs heat from the source.2. Compressor: work raises refrigerant pressure and temperature.3. Condenser: refrigerant releases useful heat.4. Expansion valve: pressure drops, ready to absorb heat again.

Energy hierarchy: Demand, Efficiency, Renewables

Use less → use well → supply cleanly. Reduce demand first, improve efficiency next, then meet remaining demand with renewable energy where suitable.

Electrical waste: WEEE means equipment

Waste Electrical and Electronic Equipment. Keep electrical waste in the correct collection stream. Some items, including mercury-containing lamps, also require hazardous-waste handling.

Test your memory

PV or solar thermal: which supplies electricity?
PV supplies electricity; solar thermal collects heat.
What changes PV d.c. into a.c.?
An inverter.
What are the two useful CHP outputs?
Heat and electrical power.
Which two water factors drive hydro output?
Flow and head: Flow × Fall.
What are the five waste hierarchy steps?
Reduce, reuse, recycle, recover, dispose.
Does grey water include toilet waste?
No. Toilet waste is excluded.
What is the heat pump cycle?
Evaporator, compressor, condenser, expansion valve.
Which Part means less energy lost?
Part L: conservation of fuel and power.
