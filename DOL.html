How to use this laboratory — quick guide
  1. Explore: rotate the 3D contactor and use START, STOP and overload demonstrations to observe the holding circuit.
  2. Investigate: choose a random fault, observe the symptoms and inspect the control and three-phase power diagrams.
  3. Measure: select the Robin tester function and range, use the simulated isolation option where required, place both probes on labelled terminals and take a reading.
  4. Diagnose and repair: choose Which Fault?, check the diagnosis, apply the simulated repair and verify START–RELEASE–STOP.
  5. Assess: complete the ten-case challenge, then preview or print the individual A4 report.

Reading guide: 0.01 Ω represents illustrative low resistance; ↑ 1 represents open circuit or over-range on the selected resistance range. Connected parallel branches can affect a continuity reading.

Safety: this is a teaching simulation. It does not perform live measurements or replace safe isolation, instrument verification or manufacturer instructions.

Three-phase power circuit — fuse, contactor, thermal overload relay (OL) and motor

Two different fuse circuits: The control fuse (FI → F) supplies the KM coil. The three power fuses (PF1I → PF1O, PF2I → PF2O, PF3I → PF3O) supply the motor phases. A failed control fuse can leave all three power fuses showing continuity.

Robin KMP 3075DL — simulated insulation and continuity tester

Place the probes directly on the labelled circuit terminals above. On the continuity/low-ohms setting, a closed contact displays 0.01 Ω; an open circuit displays ↑ 1 (over-range), matching the Robin KMP 3075DL shown in the reference photographs. Continuity readings respond to the active fault and simulated START/STOP state. Insulation resistance mode provides a clearly labelled illustrative over-range display, not a circuit-specific insulation measurement. For example, with START released its isolated resistance is ↑ 1 (over-range); while START is pressed it is 0.01 Ω unless its contact has failed. Guided mode explains the result; assessment mode hides interpretation.

Assessment challenge — ten different faults

Complete ten different faults. Each case awards up to 6 marks for a correct diagnosis, 2 for a recorded safe continuity measurement, and 2 for a fault-relevant terminal test. Each incorrect diagnosis attempt deducts 2 marks from the diagnosis portion. Revealing an answer awards zero. Testing must be completed before submitting a diagnosis.
