Last-minute recall

Explain each answer aloud before revealing it. Focus on the reason for the action, not just its name.

LO1 Safe isolation: protect yourself and everyone else
Off is not enough: isolate, secure, prove dead. Prove → test → re-prove.
LO1 Legal duties and a controlled work area
12 isolates; 13 keeps dead; 14 controls live; 16 needs competence. Safety is a work system, not just a padlock.
LO2 Record the symptom, evidence, action and result
SEAR: Symptom, Evidence, Action, Result. Write units, locations and times.
LO2 EICR codes and choosing the right document
C1 danger now; C2 danger possible; C3 improve; FI find out. Report the condition; certify the work.
LO3 Preparation: information before instruments
WHAT, WHERE, WHEN, WHAT CHANGED? Learn the normal sequence before chasing the fault.
LO3 A logical diagnosis and correction sequence
SAFE: Symptoms → Analyse → Find/repair → Evaluate. A diagnosis is a conclusion supported by evidence.
LO3 Symptoms versus causes
Symptom = what you notice. Cause = why it happened. Trip does not mean “replace breaker”.
LO3 Open circuit, short circuit and earth fault
Open breaks the route; short takes a shortcut; earth sends current onto a protective/unintended path.
LO3 High resistance joints: voltage drop and heating
Bad joint = volts lost + heat made. I²R means double current produces four times the heating at the same resistance.
LO3 Overload, inrush, leakage and protective devices
MCB: current magnitude. RCD: imbalance. RCBO: both. Identify the device before interpreting the trip.
LO3 Likely locations in wiring, accessories and controls
Trace SOURCE → PROTECTION → CONTROL → LOAD → RETURN. The dark indicator may be the fault, not the evidence.
LO3 Special precautions: lone working and hazardous areas
Special place → special plan. Control every energy source, not just the mains.
LO3 Special precautions: fibre optics, ESD and electronics
Fibre: don’t look. ESD: don’t zap. Electronics: don’t megger blindly.
LO3 Special precautions: IT, capacitors and high frequency
Mains off ≠ UPS off ≠ capacitor discharged. Calculate if needed; verify before contact.
LO3 Special precautions: batteries and transient voltages
Battery: volts may be low, current may be huge. Series adds volts. A surge is a spike, not a steady low supply.
LO4 Identify supplies and choose the right instrument
Voltage across; current through (or clamp around); resistance dead. 110 centre-tapped = 55 to earth each side.
LO4 Prove the tester before trusting the result
CASE: Condition, Accuracy, Setting, Environment. If the measurement makes no sense, verify the measurement system.
LO4 Localise the fault: split the circuit and test predictions
Predict → test → interpret. Half-split reduces the search; random part changing increases uncertainty.
LO4 Lighting and RCD diagnostic scenarios
Lamp needs line AND return. RCD trip: separate load from wiring safely, then investigate each.
LO4 DOL motor starter: control and power are separate paths
Coil makes it pull; auxiliary makes it hold; main poles feed the load. 95–96 protects, 13–14 holds.
LO5 Repair or replace: decide from evidence
SAFE repair: Suitable parts, Authorised scope, Fault cause removed, Evidence after repair.
LO5 Verify correction before restoring service
Working is not the same as safe. Safety tests + functional tests + records = verified repair.
LO5 Restore building fabric and dispose of waste safely
Repair the circuit, restore the building, remove the waste, report the outcome.
10 A through 0.5 Ω: volts lost and heat?
V = IR = 5 V; P = I²R = 50 W.
100 kΩ × 100 μF: time constant?
10 seconds. Five time constants = 50 seconds; verify actual safe voltage.
Is a tripping RCD proof of a faulty RCD?
No. Investigate leakage, neutral faults, connected loads and the device appropriately.
