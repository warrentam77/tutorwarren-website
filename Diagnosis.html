Phase 1 · Safe Isolation Procedures

Virtual training: seek permission, identify the isolation point, switch off, lock off, keep the key in your pocket, place the warning label, confirm indicator function, prove dead, then re-confirm indicator function. The optional live presence check is omitted.

Drag probe tips onto the test points, or select a probe and click its destination. Both tips must make contact.

    Model and safe-isolation guidance

    This exercise assumes one identified 230 V AC supply, a sound earth reference and no alternative supply. DB load-side checks do not prove every remote point of work dead. In practice identify all sources, secure isolation and prove dead at the actual point of work using the approved procedure. The incoming side remains live after the main switch is opened. A proving-unit check confirms the detector works; it does not prove a circuit dead.

    After testing, restore supply only after work and checks are complete, conductors and covers are secure, people are clear, and the person controlling the lock removes their own lock and warning. This phase keeps the main switch locked OFF for dead testing.

    How to use the Robin tester
    1. Complete the Safe Isolation Procedure above: seek permission, identify the isolation point, switch OFF, lock OFF, keep the key in your pocket, attach the warning, prove the voltage indicator, check L–N / L–E / N–E, then re-prove.
    2. Tick the preparation boxes below after removing the simulated lamps / loads. Insulation testing also needs sensitive equipment disconnected.
    3. Choose Ω CONTINUITY for a conductor or connection, or INSULATION RESISTANCE for insulation between conductors. For insulation practice select 2000 MΩ and turn the voltage dial to 500 V. Fault 3 uses Ω continuity for dead polarity checks.
    4. Click Red probe, then a brass screw on the board. Click Black probe, then the other terminal. Follow the two coloured leads.
    5. Press TEST. Low Ω is expected for a sound short conductor path; high MΩ is expected for sound insulation. The reading guide below explains the results.

    Try continuity from Socket A.L to Spur.L. Healthy: about 0.05 Ω. With the poor-termination scenario: about 8.05 Ω. For insulation, try Spur.L to Spur.E.

    Complete safe isolation and prepare the circuit. Select RED or BLACK, then click a brass terminal directly on the training board. The flexible lead follows your selected terminal.

    Ring ends are connected at the DB. Disconnect them for end-to-end tests.

    Scenarios and repair

    Test the two-way lighting path

    Reference wiring: C–C, L1–L1 and L2–L2. Feed enters S1.L1; the lamp return leaves S1.L2. Place red on S1.L1 and black on ceiling rose L. Hold Switch 2 on L2 and press TEST after each change: S1 on L1 gives continuity; S1 on L2 gives open circuit. Either switch reverses the complete path.

    These are connected-circuit readings. An unselected contact can still have a route through the other switch. The result is calculated from all connected conductors.

    Reading guide

    Continuity: low Ω is normally desirable; compare with expected conductor resistance. High Ω can indicate a poor termination. ↑ 1 means open circuit or above the selected range, not 1 Ω.

    Insulation: 500 V DC is simulated, with results in MΩ. This 230 V teaching circuit uses 1 MΩ as its minimum reference; a low value needs investigation. High insulation resistance does not verify continuity or the complete installation. Blue neutral and CPC must not be joined for this model.

    Operate lighting switches in both positions to include both paths. Separate ring ends for end-to-end continuity. Values are illustrative; no external parallel paths, appliances or capacitance are included. A successful test does not certify an installation.

    Fault-finding scenario 1 · Ring final circuit

    Job brief: You are carrying out continuity tests on the B32 ring final circuit, including its spur. A continuity reading at the spur socket is unexpectedly high compared with the other socket outlets. The cause has not been identified.
    Your task: Use the Robin tester to investigate the circuit, locate the fault and explain your evidence. Recommend a repair, apply your chosen virtual fix, then repeat the relevant tests to confirm whether the fault has been cleared.

    Start the investigation to load this job. The fault location and type are hidden. Safe-isolation progress is retained.

    1. Complete safe isolation and prepare the circuit.
    2. Plan your continuity checks. Compare readings at the DB, ring sockets and spur; narrow the problem down by testing smaller sections. Click an accessory to inspect its terminations.
    3. Select the faulty location and explain what your readings show.
    4. Repair the connection virtually, then repeat the same test to verify the result.
