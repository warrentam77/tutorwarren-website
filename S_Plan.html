Original wiring • animated live paths

Switch blades close when their controls make contact. Gold highlights = live voltage, which can be present without current flow. White markers indicate an enabled control path, not AC electron direction or measured current. No markers cross an open end switch or travel along the inactive orange branch. Blue neutral and green/yellow protective conductors retain their original colours.

Select a scenario, then change either thermostat to see what happens when a zone is satisfied. Valve travel is shortened to 4 seconds for teaching.

The supplied drawing calls the hot-water control “Boiler Stat”. Its position in this S Plan control circuit is the hot-water cylinder thermostat. This animation uses that functional name.

Why can an inactive valve’s orange wire be live? Both orange wires join at terminal 10. A closed end switch on either valve makes the entire orange connection live, including the other valve’s orange wire. The open end switch prevents current through that switch. The valve motor is a separate brown-to-blue circuit, so the shared orange live does not power the inactive motor. Its orange branch is highlighted for voltage only, without moving markers.

This models the simple boiler/pump circuit shown. Modern boilers may require permanent live and manufacturer-controlled pump overrun. Valve opening/closing times and end-switch release points vary; here the end switch opens as the valve leaves the fully-open position. Educational simulation only: isolate and prove dead before practical work on 230 V wiring.

EAL • Level 3 Electrical Installation Craft Skills • 3-05B3

Heating system craft assessment

A teaching and practical learning companion for the S Plan heating-system task. Source: Practical Assessments, Issue 3, 08/13, pages 20–26; general assessment guidance on pages 3–4. This is the heating assessment only. The source requires successful completion of lighting (3-05B1), motor circuit (3-05B2) and heating (3-05B3) assessments to achieve the complete unit.

Practical assessment: 4 hours. All components are pre-fixed. The learner must not be able to energise the installation. Stop the assessment if unsafe practice is observed. The animation is a teaching aid; completing it does not award a practical pass. Use this 2013 pack alongside your centre’s applicable assessment requirements and current technical references.

1 · Prepare

Read the learner brief and inspect the unwired rig layout. Identify the boiler, circulating pump, programmer, room thermostat, hot-water cylinder thermostat and two 2-port zone valves. Select suitable tools, cable, equipment and PPE; have the IET On-Site Guide available.

2 · Complete the drawing

On the incomplete diagram, add connections for the boiler, hot-water thermostat and hot-water zone valve. Ask the assessor to check your completed diagram before starting the wiring.

3 · Wire and inspect

Wire the pre-fixed rig to a good commercial standard using your checked diagram and relevant technical information. Work safely, keep the area tidy and inspect conductor identification, terminations and connections against the specification.
