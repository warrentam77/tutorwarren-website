Mind the traps

  • Dead tests come first; live tests (Zs, PFC, RCD) come last.
  • If a test fails, rectify and repeat it plus any earlier tests affected by the fault or repair.
  • 1 MΩ insulation resistance complies, but under 2 MΩ needs investigation.
  • GS38 is HSE guidance on test equipment. It is not a regulation.

Last-minute recall: explain it before revealing

Spend 10–15 minutes on these prompts, then revisit the notes for anything you cannot explain. Focus on purpose, instrument, method and interpretation.

LO1 Safe isolation: identify, secure and prove dead
Permission → identify → off → lock → key → label → prove → dead → re-prove. A padlock prevents reconnection; the indicator confirms absence of voltage.
LO1 Law, competence and protecting other people
12 isolates; 13 keeps dead; 14 justifies live; 16 needs competence. Live work: ALL three conditions, never just one.
LO1 GS38 and instrument checks
CASE: Condition, Accuracy, Setting, Environment. Correct instrument + correct setting + correct connection.
LO2 Initial verification, periodic inspection and information
Initial = before service. Periodic = condition in service. Information before instruments.
LO3 Inspection: what a meter cannot tell you
CABLES: Connections, Access, Bonding, Labelling, Equipment, Support. Inspect the construction; test the electrical performance.
LO4 Test order, instruments and failed results
Continuity wants LOW Ω; insulation wants HIGH MΩ. Dead before live; rectify before certify.
LO4 Protective conductor continuity and R₁ + R₂
R₁ = line, R₂ = protective return. Milliohms to ohms: divide by 1000. LO4 Ring final circuits: the three-step method
Ring = THREE loops, THREE steps: ends → L/N cross → L/E cross. Add the two end-to-end values, then divide by FOUR.
LO4 Insulation resistance: method, voltages and interpretation
250 / 500 / 1000 volts; 0.5 / 1 / 1 megohms. High insulation resistance is good; discharge before disconnecting.IR example: joined live conductors to protective conductorLNPEMΩDead circuit. LO4 Cable length and parallel insulation paths
Longer conductor = more resistance. More insulation leakage paths = less insulation resistance. Equal parallel paths: divide by their number.
LO4 Polarity and special protective measures
Polarity = put the switch in LINE. SELV: separated and not intentionally earthed; PELV permits earthing.
LO4 Earthing systems and the fault-current path
S = separate; C = combined; TT = two earth connections with soil between. Trace the loop back to the transformer.
LO4 Earth electrodes and TT protection
TT: resistance × residual current ≤ touch-voltage limit. 30 mA = 0.03 A. Spikes measure the electrode; a loop test measures a loop.
LO4 Zₑ, Zₛ and automatic disconnection of supply
Zₑ = external; Zₛ = sum. High loop impedance → lower fault current → potentially slower disconnection.
LO4 Prospective fault current and device capacity
Fault current: divide VOLTS by OHMS. Breaking capacity is in kA; normal circuit rating is in A.
LO4 RCD testing: textbook figures and modern verification
RCD units: mA triggers it; ms times it. 1×30 = 30 mA; 5×30 = 150 mA. Device characteristic ≠ mandatory test sequence.
LO4 Phase sequence, functional tests and commissioning
Sequence sets direction; function proves operation; commissioning hands over a working system.
LO4 Voltage drop: calculate, compare and improve
mV needs ÷1000. Lighting THREE; other FIVE. Use design current, length and the correct factor.
LO5 Certificates, schedules and responsibilities
EIC = new work; Minor = existing circuit alteration; EICR = existing condition. Inspection tells what was seen; test schedule tells what was measured.
LO5 Periodic reports, intervals and client handover
C1 danger now; C2 danger possible; C3 improve; FI find out. Recommend the interval from evidence, not just a table.

Can a lamp work with incorrect polarity?
Yes. Operation does not prove correct polarity.
